# Research scope

## Primary domains

### 1. Quality standards

Compare USP–NF and Ph. Eur. only when the same material type, dosage form, analyte, unit, and basis are sufficiently aligned.

Possible outputs:

- equivalent;
- USP more restrictive for a matched numeric parameter;
- Ph. Eur. more restrictive for a matched numeric parameter;
- different limits;
- different method;
- USP-only test;
- Ph. Eur.-only test;
- not directly comparable.

These labels describe standards, not clinical superiority.

### 2. Regulatory clinical evidence

FDA and EMA documents are first classified by evidence role. Only suitable primary/summary clinical assessments are candidates for direct comparison. Supporting safety, pharmacology, product-information, and administrative documents remain visible but are not treated as equivalent efficacy assessments.

### 3. HTA/value

NICE is represented as a separate HTA/value layer. Recommendation, relative effectiveness, cost-effectiveness, commercial/managed-entry signals, and restrictions should not be conflated with FDA/EMA approval conclusions.

### 4. Independent clinical studies

Study PDFs provide candidate effect estimates for endpoint-specific evidence synthesis. Pooled analyses require manual comparability verification.

## Example research questions

- How often do matched USP and Ph. Eur. monographs differ in test coverage, numeric acceptance criteria, or analytical method?
- How often are FDA and EMA clinical assessments aligned in indication, population, comparator, and major endpoint interpretation?
- Which fields are most reliably extracted by deterministic rules vs local LLM enrichment?
- How does extraction accuracy vary by document type?
- Can a provenance-preserving hybrid extraction system reduce manual evidence-structuring effort while maintaining auditable error rates?

Cross-domain association analysis can be exploratory, but the software should not imply that pharmacopoeial restrictiveness causes better clinical outcomes.
